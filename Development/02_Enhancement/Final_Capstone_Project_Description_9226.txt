1 Overview
The final capstone is an integrated assessment of business analytics, business intelligence, Artificial
Intelligence and executive communication. Each team acts as an executive analytics advisory group, using
its assigned enterprise dataset to investigate a business problem and develop recommendations that a
management audience can assess.
The project follows a decision-focused approach. Students should begin with the decisions and business
questions, establish trustworthy measures, examine performance and evaluate suitable AI applications.
Analytical complexity is useful only when it helps answer those questions and the team can explain its
results and limitations.
Group 9226 comprises five thematic project teams. The portfolio covers enterprise financial
performance, quality in asset-intensive projects, predictive maintenance, resource allocation and digital
transformation. Each team applies the same analytical principles while preserving the operating and
financial definitions of its assigned scenario.
The supplied enterprise datasets are fully synthetic educational panels with embedded business rules,
operational relationships and derived indicators. Their formulas and simulated patterns support
analytical exercises; they are not observed company outcomes. Correlation alone does not establish
causation, and predictive performance does not demonstrate achieved savings or operational
improvement.
Students will produce a working Power BI dashboard, an executive presentation and a comprehensive
analytical report. The supplied reference publication explains the case and dataset; the submitted
capstone must contain the team’s own validation, analysis, implemented dashboard, model evaluation
and recommendations.

2 Learning Outcomes
On successful completion, students should be able to demonstrate the following capabilities across the
enterprise analytics lifecycle:
• Translate a business scenario into explicit executive questions and decision objectives.
• Interpret the assigned dataset, its grain, dimensions, information timing and synthetic assumptions.
• Validate completeness, uniqueness, business rules, accounting identities and KPI formulas.
• Conduct systematic exploratory data analysis of distributions, trends, relationships and anomalies.
• Engineer and validate derived measures with correct denominators and financial scope.
• Build interactive Power BI dashboards with a coherent data model, navigation and drill-through.
• Connect descriptive and diagnostic analysis with suitable predictive or prescriptive applications.
• Select and evaluate AI methods against informative baselines using appropriate validation.
• Explain model performance, uncertainty and practical limits in business language.
• Develop recommendations that recognize operating constraints and the evidence needed for
implementation.
• Communicate findings through a professional report and a concise executive briefing.
• Use AI tools responsibly, disclose their contribution and retain human responsibility for the final
judgement.
Evidence of learning
The dashboard, report and presentation should tell the same business story. KPI definitions, totals,
model metrics and recommendations must reconcile across all three deliverables. Every team member
should be able to explain the decisions, evidence and limitations, including the parts developed by other
members.

Assign clear responsibilities, maintain a common analytical file structure and record the work completed

4 Project Scope
4.1 Dataset Governance
Establish the analytical grain and data dictionary before calculating executive results. Check row and
variable counts, missing values, duplicates, data types, dimensional consistency and date coverage.
Validate operational conservation, inventory continuity and financial formulas where applicable.
• Record the checks performed and summarize passed checks and unresolved exceptions.
• Distinguish source-quality measures from missing values in the canonical dataset.
• Document zero-denominator handling, repeated counts and exclusions from financial measures.
• Retain a traceable transformation workflow and reconcile results to the assigned master.
The instructor’s internal dataset engineering process is separate from the student submission. The
student report should present its own governance methodology and evidence, not reproduce the
internal engineering workflow as a publication section.
4.2 Exploratory Data Analysis
Investigate distributions, operational and financial trends, customer or account patterns where
represented, correlations, outliers, anomalies and executive indicators. Use business interpretation to
explain the results and their significance for the assigned decisions.
• Use appropriate descriptive statistics and disclose the relevant populations and periods.
• Review performance by enterprise dimensions and compare like-for-like units.
• Distinguish accounting or operational identities from independent associations.
• Investigate outliers before removal; preserve valid exceptional observations.
• Avoid interpreting fictional geography or simulated relationships as measured real-world evidence.
EDA provides the foundation for dashboard measures, feature selection and modelling. Include clear
evidence of how exploratory findings informed later analytical choices.

4 Project Scope Continued
4.3 Data Transformation and Feature Engineering
Prepare the data for Power BI and any selected AI method. Set appropriate data types, construct
dimensions, document transformations and validate engineered variables. Derive executive KPIs with
explicit formulas, business meanings and portfolio aggregation rules.
Use matching summed numerators and denominators where appropriate. Do not average row rates
without considering their weights, double-count linked indicators or sum repeated entity counts as
unique customers or employees. Distinguish predictors available before a decision from outcomes
observed afterward.
4.4 Executive Dashboard Development
Build executive-ready Power BI views with KPI scorecards, interactive slicers, navigation, drill-through,
trends and comparative analysis. Geographic views are appropriate only where meaningful locations are
provided. Use consistent currency, units, labels and visual formatting.
The project reference contains five suggested dashboard designs. Adapt and implement suitable views
within a coherent PBIX file. The requirement for at least five visualization types is distinct from the
number of dashboard pages; choose page count and layout to support the assigned business decisions.
4.5 Artificial Intelligence Applications
Investigate suitable classification, clustering, regression, forecasting, recommendation or prioritization
methods. Select methods because they answer a business question; every listed technique need not be
applied. Each selected model requires business justification, transparent inputs, evaluation and executive
interpretation.
Compare predictive models with an informative baseline. For time-dependent scenarios, use
chronological validation and prevent current-outcome leakage. Report relevant metrics, error patterns
and limitations. Clustering requires a clear profile interpretation and a stability or suitability assessment.
State whether an application was executed or only proposed.
4.6 Executive Recommendations
Recommendations should address the identified problems, cite analytical evidence, recognize constraints
and identify a practical owner or next step. Separate demonstrated findings from proposed actions and
unvalidated impact estimates. Optimization claims require explicit objectives, feasible constraints and a
solved comparison; descriptive rankings alone do not establish an optimal decision.

6 Executive Presentation Requirements
Each team will deliver an executive briefing of approximately 15 minutes. The session should simulate a
meeting with senior management or a board. Presentation scheduling and any separate question period
will be announced by the instructors.
Executive briefing expectations
• Define the management problem and the decision your analysis supports.
• Demonstrate the dashboard and explain how its views answer the business questions.
• Present the strongest numerical findings with clear business meaning.
• Explain the selected AI application, baseline comparison and performance limits.
• Prioritize practical recommendations and disclose constraints and unvalidated benefits.
• Respond to questions about data definitions, measures, model choices and team judgement.
• Manage time and coordinate transitions between presenters.
Professional communication
Assessment considers executive presence, clarity, confidence, storytelling, business relevance,
evidence-based recommendations, time management and response to questions. Technical detail should
be included where it supports a management decision, with terminology explained for the intended
audience.

9 Responsible AI and AI Usage Disclosure
AI tools may support brainstorming, coding, documentation, visualization and drafting. Each team
remains responsible for interpreting the dataset, selecting models, validating calculations, exercising
business judgement and producing its final recommendations and conclusions.
Required disclosure
Include an AI Usage Disclosure Statement in the report that specifies:
• The AI tools used.
• The purpose of each use and the scope of assistance.
• The tasks and content generated or revised with AI support.
• The checks performed by team members to validate calculations, code, sources and conclusions.
• Confirmation that final analytical judgement and responsibility remain with the project team.
Distinguish a predictive or analytical model from a generative AI assistant. Explain both where used. Do
not present AI-generated text, invented figures or unverified model results as analytical evidence.
Preserve the synthetic provenance of the assigned data and acknowledge the limits of generalization.
10 Final Guidance
The capstone demonstrates how a management team can turn a defined business problem and
trustworthy analytical data into an executive discussion. Strong work combines validated measures,
appropriate analysis, a usable dashboard, honest model evaluation and recommendations that respect
the evidence.
Use the assigned enterprise framework consistently, but adapt the interpretation to the scenario. Explain
what happened in the synthetic setting, what management should investigate and what additional
information is needed before acting. A descriptive association is not a proven causal effect, a forecast is
not an optimized plan, and proposed benefits are not realized gains.
Maintain an integrated team workflow, review all deliverables together and prepare to defend the
reasoning behind the conclusions. Every visual should answer a business question, every selected model
should support a decision and every recommendation should state its evidence and practical limits.
